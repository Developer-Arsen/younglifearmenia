How it started

Our Story

It began with a handful of adults who made time for teenagers, and kept making it.

In 301 AD Armenia became the first country in the world to embrace Christianity as its national faith. After the Soviet decades of the 20th century, the country regained independence in 1991 and returned to its spiritual heritage.

Young Life Armenia works in that inheritance. Leaders spend months, sometimes years, with the same young people — at school, at club, on the way to camp — and in those friendships talk plainly about Jesus. Nobody is asked to agree with anything to be welcome.

The camp

Pioneer Camp

A week in the mountains: sport, hiking, meals together, and an honest conversation about faith.

In the mountains, with friends

Pioneer Camp stands at Artavaz, on the Marmarik River, about ninety minutes from Yerevan. Groups come for a week at a time — dorms, shared meals, and a valley with very little else in it.

Days are for hiking, sport and games. Evenings are quieter: a short talk about who Jesus is, then time to sit with it, ask about it or argue with it.

Camp history

Then, transformation, today

Choose a stage and see how the camp has changed.

the Soviet years

A former Soviet camp

This place was once a gathering ground for Soviet youth, where Armenian teenagers were taught atheism and communism.

the refurbishment

The transformation

Young Life rebuilt the site as a Christian camp. It now serves close to the opposite of the purpose it was built for.

today

The camp today

Today it hosts groups from across Armenia through the summer and winter programmes. For a good number of them it is the first place the Christian faith is explained to them rather than argued at them.

The most anticipated day of summer

Water Games

  • WATER ·
  • LAUGHTER ·
  • FRIENDSHIP

One afternoon each summer when the whole camp ends up on the same field, soaked.

Buckets, water balloons, a garden hose and a few paddling pools. Nobody stays dry.

When it is over everyone gathers on the field for one photograph.

Once every summer

Colour Day

Everyone starts the afternoon in a white shirt. Nobody finishes in one.

Powder paint by the bucket, shaving foam, a hosepipe and whatever the kitchen can spare. It runs for one afternoon and takes the rest of the evening to wash out.

It is an easy day for a newcomer. After twenty minutes everybody looks the same, and the quietest person on the field is in every photograph with the rest.

  • A white shirt nobody loves
  • Powder paint, by the bucket
  • Shaving foam
  • One hosepipe, running

On the field

Relays & Team Games

Team games on the field, most days of the week.

Every day brings a few contests on the field: a hoop relay, walking on wooden stilts, hand-offs, and games that simply do not work without a team.

Scores are forgotten by dinner. Teams are mixed on purpose, so campers spend the afternoon alongside people they did not arrive with.

  • Team
  • Speed
  • Trust
  • Plenty of laughing

Every week, not only in summer

Club Nights

Camp is one week of the year. Club is the other fifty-one.

A club night is loud on purpose: songs, a game that makes very little sense, and leaders on stage who were campers themselves a few years ago.

Near the end the room goes quiet for about ten minutes while someone gives a short talk about Jesus. Nobody is asked to say anything back.

  • Songs
  • Games
  • A story
  • Ten quiet minutes
